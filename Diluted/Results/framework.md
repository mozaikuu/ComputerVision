GA-Optimized VGG16 Framework
Figure X. Workflow of the GA-optimized VGG16 training pipeline. First, a pretrained VGG16 model is loaded and its final dense layer replaced with a new output layer for pneumonia classification. A genetic algorithm then initializes a population of candidate network configurations (e.g. variations in layer parameters). Through iterative loops of selection and mutation with fitness evaluation on a validation set, the GA searches the space of architectures. Ultimately the fittest model is chosen as the final classifier (adapted from generic evolutionary flowcharts). Figure X illustrates the end-to-end pipeline. We start with a pretrained VGG16 CNN (left) and swap its ImageNet head for a binary pneumonia-output layer. The GA (middle) generates an initial population of variants (different filter sets, layer arrangements, etc.) and repeatedly applies crossover and mutation to produce new generations. Each candidate is briefly trained and scored by validation accuracy (fitness). Over successive generations, poorly performing variants are discarded and the search concentrates around high-performing configurations. Finally, the best-evolved model is selected (right) for full training. This framework combines transfer learning with evolutionary hyperparameter search, as also discussed in prior work on automated CNN design.
